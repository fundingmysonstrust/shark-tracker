How to read this

Simple on the surface.

What's a shark?A trader with a strong, long, profitable track record on Polymarket. Score 60+ out of 100 and at least 20 finished markets.
What does 34¢ mean?A share pays $1 if that side wins. 34¢ means the market gives it about a 34% chance.
What's agreement?The share of sharks on the bigger side. 90% means 9 out of 10 sharks hold the same side.
Is this advice?No. It shows what other traders hold, using public data. Sharks can be wrong.

What this site shows

It finds consistently profitable traders ("sharks") on Polymarket's public leaderboards, looks at the bets they currently hold, and counts how many sharks hold each side of each open market. It is a report only: it doesn't recommend anything and never places trades.

Columns

  • Market: the question, in Polymarket's own words. Click any row to see every shark, their bet size, entry price and score.
  • Category: sports, politics, economics, finance, crypto, culture, tech, weather, mentions or other.
  • Event date: game start time for sports, otherwise the date the market closes. US Eastern time.
  • Side A / side B: how many sharks hold each answer (for example "Yes" vs "No", or Team 1 vs Team 2), with their names.
  • Total: sharks on side A vs side B. Sharks holding both sides are "hedged" and not counted for either.
  • Agreement: share of sharks on the bigger side. 100% = all on one side; 50% = an even split.
  • Shark $ now: what the sharks' bets on each side are worth right now.
  • Avg shark entry: the average price the sharks paid, in cents (100¢ = $1 if that side wins).
  • Price now: today's Polymarket price for side A / side B.
  • Kalshi price: same market on Kalshi, when one is found. Kalshi doesn't publish other people's bets, so there are no Kalshi shark counts.
  • Flags: "Split" = sharks are divided (e.g. 6 vs 5).
  • Possible matches: when a Kalshi market looks like the same question but the computer isn't sure, it's listed above the table. Press "Same market" or "Not the same"; your answer is remembered.

Who counts as a shark

Each trader gets a 0–100 reliability score per category from: 30-day profit, all-time profit, win rate, number of settled markets, profit ÷ money traded, and being profitable both recently and all-time. 60+ = shark (you can change this on Settings). Click a name on the Sharks tab to see the full breakdown.

If the site says the data is stale

  1. Wait 15 minutes and reload the page. Most problems fix themselves on the next refresh.
  2. If it still says stale after an hour, look at the status boxes at the top. They say which source failed.
  3. The site always keeps showing the last good data, so nothing is lost.
  4. If it's been stale for more than a day, start a chat with Claude and paste the message from the status box.
